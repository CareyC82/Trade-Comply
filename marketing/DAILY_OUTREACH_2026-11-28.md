## Source verification — foreign IOR / CTPAT broker requirement

- CBP's Nov. 6, 2026 interim final rule, “Heightened Requirements for Foreign Importers of Record,” states it takes effect Jan. 5, 2027; comments are due that date. It establishes requirements for foreign IORs and related customs-broker responsibilities.
- Under 19 CFR 141.21(b)(2) as adopted in the rule, a foreign IOR that is not CTPAT-validated must designate a licensed customs broker that is CTPAT-validated to make entry or act as IOR, subject to the rule’s conditions. A CTPAT broker’s use does not give CTPAT benefits to the IOR.
- CBP allows customs brokers to request delayed compliance only for the new client-verification requirement; a request must be submitted by Jan. 5 and is subject to CBP approval. It is not an automatic extension of the foreign-IOR rule.
- Primary source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-06/html/2026-22735.htm
- CBP's current CTPAT validated-broker listing was refreshed Nov. 27, 2026: https://ace-accounts.cbp.gov/s/customs-broker-listing
- Recheck the Federal Register/CBP on the day of posting for updates, court action, or implementation guidance.

**Does a U.S.-bound DDP quote tell you who the importer of record will be? Not by itself.**

CBP's interim final rule on foreign importers of record takes effect January 5, 2027. It creates a specific condition when a foreign entity itself is the IOR: CBP says the foreign IOR must be CTPAT-validated to enter goods on its own; otherwise, it must designate a licensed customs broker that is CTPAT-validated to make entry on its behalf. Depending on the underlying transaction, the broker may need to act as IOR if the foreign party is not the owner or purchaser.

That is not the same as saying every DDP quote is noncompliant or that a seller's address alone identifies the IOR. The key first question is which legal entity will actually be shown as IOR on the entry.

Before approving a U.S.-bound quote, ask:
• Which legal entity will be the importer of record?
• Which licensed broker will make entry, and has the broker confirmed its role and CTPAT validation for this arrangement?
• Who will provide and retain the records needed for entry?

Rule text: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-06/html/2026-22735.htm

TraceWize is an early-stage product-and-route pre-screen. It does not determine IOR status, provide customs brokerage, or give legal advice.

If a quote says DDP, do you already know which entity will appear as IOR?
