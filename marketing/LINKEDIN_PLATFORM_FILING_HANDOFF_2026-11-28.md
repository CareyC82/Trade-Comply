## Copy

“The platform supports CPSC filing” is a useful start—but it may not answer who is responsible for this shipment.

TikTok Shop’s U.S. guide says Full Service sellers receive filing support, while some other seller models receive education because importer-of-record responsibilities differ.

For a specific order or inbound shipment, separate three questions:

1. Does this exact product need a CPSC certificate at all?
2. Which legal entity is the importer of record?
3. Who will transmit the certificate data through the customs entry—and has that handoff been confirmed for this seller program and shipment?

Platform review, filing support, and a customs entry are not the same thing. A seller should not infer the importer or filer just from a marketplace name, fulfillment label, or DDP quote.

TraceWize is an early-stage product-and-route pre-screen that helps organize those questions before broker, platform, or specialist review. It does not file entries or decide legal responsibility.

If you sell small electronics cross-border, what part of the platform-to-broker handoff is least clear today?

## Accuracy notes

- TikTok's seller-role guidance applies to its own U.S. seller programs; do not imply all platforms follow the same support model.
- A platform's filing support does not itself prove who is importer of record or that a specific shipment's data was transmitted.
- CPSC eFiling changes how certificate data is submitted when a product is already subject to a certificate requirement; it does not make all electronics require a CPSC certificate.
- The CBP postal rule is cited only as background on specific postal entry paths, not as evidence for non-postal marketplace shipments.
