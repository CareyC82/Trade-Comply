# LinkedIn draft — EU product identifiers for low-value distance sales

Status: Draft only; proposed Dec. 6 slot; source-check on publication day.

## Draft

**Selling products online to EU consumers from outside the EU? Product identity now needs to travel with the customs data.**

From November 1, 2026, EU customs declarations for imported goods sold in distance sales with an intrinsic value not exceeding €150 require product identifiers. The European Commission describes three distinct identifiers:

- the merchant product identifier (M-PID), such as the seller or platform's unique product reference;
- the manufacturer's non-standardised product identifier (NS-PID), such as its model or part number; and
- the standardised identifier (S-PID), such as an EAN or UPC, where one exists. If there is no standardised identifier, the declaration needs the applicable exception code.

These are not interchangeable fields. A seller SKU can identify the offer in a store, while the manufacturer’s model/part number links the product to the maker and model. A generic description alone is not the same thing.

Before your next eligible shipment, check that the exact product/variant, seller reference, manufacturer model and barcode status are captured consistently in your listing and can be passed to the party filing the customs declaration. Confirm the required data format with your marketplace or customs representative.

This is a customs-data identification requirement; it does not itself certify product safety or establish that the product is allowed to be sold. TraceWize is an early-stage product-and-market pre-screen, not a customs filer or legal adviser.

Official European Commission overview: https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/product-identifiers-e-commerce-imports-mandatory-1-november-2026-2026-11-03_en
