## 1. On OIA Global’s recent post about U.S. address/EIN and IOR status

Helpful distinction. A U.S. mailing address or EIN alone does not settle whether the entity qualifies as a U.S. IOR under the new rule. I’d start by confirming which legal entity will actually appear as IOR on the entry and asking the broker to confirm the arrangement—especially for goods that may clear on or after January 5. ## 2. On Brian Bateman’s FCC marketplace-ID post

Useful reminder to separate the marketplace display timeline from the product’s authorization record. For a specific listing, the seller still needs to confirm that the FCC ID applies to the exact model; where the ID belongs to a radio module, the grant’s host-integration conditions matter too. An FCC database match by itself does not prove listing applicability. eBay’s seller guide: https://www.ebay.com/sellercenter/resources/fcc-id

Post: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/splinternetmarketing_fcc-expands-equipment-security-rules-and-activity-7505234566761140224-euMC

## 3. On Nic Gong’s recent post about evidence being disconnected from the current product version

Strong point on version linkage. For an imported product that is subject to a CPSC certificate requirement, I’d keep two checks separate: whether this exact product requires a certificate, and whether the certificate data/evidence corresponds to the version actually entering. CPSC eFiling changes how required certificate data is submitted; it does not make every product subject to CPSC certification. Keeping scope and version identity separate may help teams spot a real gap without treating a generic document folder as proof the entry is ready.

Official seller guidance: https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GWCJUJ9WUZNRYVGN?itemid=200280130&locale=en-us
