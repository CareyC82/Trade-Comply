# LinkedIn post draft — DDP and foreign IOR (Nov. 29 slot)

**Status:** Draft only. Manual publication; use only if Nov. 28's CPSC post is actually published. Recheck CBP/Amazon guidance on publication day.

## Copy

If a U.S.-bound supplier quote says DDP, it still may not tell you which legal entity will be named as importer of record.

CBP's foreign-importer rule takes effect January 5, 2027. When a foreign company is the IOR, it generally must be CTPAT-validated to enter goods on its own. If it is not validated, the rule requires a licensed customs broker that is CTPAT-validated to make entry on its behalf; the broker must act as IOR in certain cases where the foreign party is not the owner or purchaser. Confirm the exact arrangement with the broker—don't infer it from a quote or shipping label.

Timing matters too: CBP's rule is effective January 5, and Amazon Seller Central warns that the rule applies to goods clearing U.S. customs on or after that date, including goods already in transit. Shipping before January 5 may not avoid the new entry requirements.

Before dispatch, ask:

• Which legal entity will be named as IOR on this entry?
• Which licensed broker will file, and has it confirmed whether CTPAT validation is required for this arrangement?
• Who will own the entry records and confirm the final IOR/filer before the shipment departs?

## Accuracy notes

- CBP sets the effective date. Amazon's seller guidance is an operational reminder for its sellers, not CBP law.
- Do not claim every DDP shipment is noncompliant or that the seller's address alone determines IOR status.
- Broker-delayed compliance is a separate CBP process and is not automatic; don't suggest it applies to sellers.
- The rule is interim final. Recheck for changes before publication.
