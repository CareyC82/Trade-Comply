### Recommended LinkedIn post for today (CPSC eFiling handoff; source-checked)

CPSC eFiling: the rule is live, but the product-data handoff still matters.

Since July 8, 2026, covered consumer-product imports that require a CPSC certificate must eFile the certificate data. This changes how the data is sent; CPSC says it creates no new testing or certification obligation, and it does not apply to every product.

A practical pre-entry check:
• Does this exact product require a CPSC certificate?
• Do the certificate and exact model/variant match?
• Who can confirm the product facts and supply the filing data?
• What does the broker need, and by when?

CPSC says the eFiling importer may be the owner, purchaser, or an authorized customs broker. If the broker lacks enough product knowledge to take responsibility for testing and certification, CPSC guidance says it may identify the owner, purchaser, or consignee who authorized the entry as responsible for the certificate requirements.

What tends to slow this handoff down most: identifying the applicable certificate, matching it to the exact model, or getting the data to the broker in time?

CPSC importer guidance: https://www.cpsc.gov/efiling/importers

Still comparing a product with a new destination? Try TraceWize's no-account preliminary product-market screen: https://tracewize.com/can-i-sell-it.html. It helps organize questions before specialist review; it does not file customs entries or issue compliance decisions.

### Foreign importer-of-record post (future LinkedIn post; fact-checked, recheck before publishing)

If a company outside the U.S. will act as importer of record for U.S.-bound goods, confirm the customs-broker arrangement before the next entry.

CBP’s interim final rule takes effect January 5, 2027. It applies to consumption entries where formal entry is filed and to specified informal entries (operationally, entry types 11, 12, and 13)—not to every seller or shipment.

Under the rule, a foreign IOR that is not CTPAT-validated must designate a licensed customs broker that is CTPAT-validated to make the entry or act as IOR, subject to the rule’s specific conditions. A broker may request delayed compliance with its new client-verification requirement; if CBP approves a request, full broker compliance is still expected by May 6, 2027. The delay is not automatic.

Before shipping, confirm:
• Which entity will be the IOR?
• What entry type will be filed?
• Is the foreign IOR CTPAT-validated, or will a CTPAT-validated broker handle the entry?
• Has the broker clarified its own compliance timeline?

The rule is an interim final rule, with comments due January 5, 2027. Recheck current CBP guidance and the Federal Register before relying on it for a specific shipment.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-06/html/2026-22735.htm

### Third-party compliance provider checklist (future LinkedIn post; not published)

Marketplace directories can make it easier to find compliance support—but “listed” and “endorsed” are not the same thing.

eBay says it reviews providers for relevance before listing them, while also making clear that providers are independent and eBay does not manage or endorse their services.

Before engaging any provider, ask:

• Does the service cover this exact product type and destination?
• Will they review existing reports, or is new testing potentially needed?
• What written deliverable, timeline, and fee are included?
• Which decisions and filings remain the seller’s responsibility?

Start with the exact model/variant, product configuration, destination, and the platform notice or question. That makes it easier to tell whether the provider’s scope matches the problem.

### CPSC eFiling handoff (future post)

Since July 8, CPSC eFiling has applied to most imported consumer products that already require a CPSC certificate. It changes how certificate data is transmitted; CPSC says it does not create a new testing or certification obligation, and it is not a blanket requirement for every product.

The operational question many teams should settle before the next entry is: who can confirm the product facts and certificate data that go into the filing?

CPSC says the eFiling importer may be the owner, purchaser, or authorized customs broker eligible to make entry. If a broker does not have enough product knowledge to take responsibility for testing and certification, CPSC guidance says the broker may identify the owner, purchaser, or consignee who authorized the entry as the responsible party for certificate requirements.

Before shipment, align the product/model, applicable certificate, responsible compliance party, and the handoff to the broker. Whether eFiling applies depends on whether the product is subject to a CPSC certificate requirement.

CPSC importer guidance: https://www.cpsc.gov/efiling/importers
CPSC applicability guide: https://www.cpsc.gov/efiling/help
