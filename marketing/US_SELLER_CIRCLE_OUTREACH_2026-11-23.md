## If an organizer agrees: 15-minute format

1. **2 min — frame the gap:** Before placing another order, a seller needs to know which product facts, market and channel they are actually assessing. The same generic product label can conceal differences in radio/battery/power configuration and evidence.
2. **5 min — generic example:** Use a fictional Bluetooth speaker or charger; compare one U.S. and one additional destination selected by attendees. Keep the product facts intentionally incomplete, then show how “unknown” should remain unknown rather than be guessed.
3. **4 min — classify the next action:** Supplier evidence / seller's own platform account / customs-broker or lab confirmation. Explicitly distinguish pre-screening from certification, legal advice, account appeal, or approval.
4. **3 min — attendee feedback:** Ask which fact would have changed a purchase decision, which result was unclear, and whether they would return before the next sourcing decision.
5. **1 min — optional CTA:** Offer the Can I Sell It? page only if the organizer approves. Invite a small number to test it with non-confidential product facts; do not collect documents live.

**Useful success criteria:** at least three attendees have a real upcoming sourcing/destination decision; at least one voluntarily completes a screen after the session; record what action the pre-screen changed. Attendance, impressions, and link clicks alone are not product-market validation.

## 15-minute seller test protocol — only after moderator permission

1. Ask for a seller with a real consumer-electronics market-entry/sourcing decision in the next 90 days; exclude professional compliance consultants for this first seller-specific test.
2. Before screen share, say this is early-stage product research, not certification/legal advice, and participation is optional. Do not ask for account credentials, ASINs, supplier names, confidential reports, or personal information.
3. Ask the seller to complete one real or anonymized example, narrating what they expect the result to tell them. Note only task success/failure and aggregate themes; ask permission separately before retaining a quote or recording.
4. Ask: “What decision, if any, would you make differently after this?” “What did you expect but not find?” “What would make you return for the next product?”
5. Success signal is not praise: at least 2 of 3 can finish without coaching, name a concrete changed/confirmed next action, and say they would return before another sourcing decision. Otherwise capture the blocking point for Project Development to assess; no code change until Carey approves.
