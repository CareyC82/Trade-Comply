# TraceWize US Market Launch Pack

## Positioning

**Category:** Global trade compliance pre-screening for US-based teams.

**Primary message:**

> Your office is in the US. Your trade routes are global.

**Supporting message:**

> TraceWize helps US-based trade, sourcing, procurement, and operations teams pre-screen route-specific import, export, tariff, product, and documentation risks before they buy, sell, or ship.

Do not position TraceWize as a US-only import tool or a China-only sourcing tool. The customer and decision team may be in the United States while goods move between third countries.

## Initial ICP

- US-headquartered or US-operated companies with global suppliers, customers, or factories.
- 10–500 employees or a small global-trade team inside a larger company.
- Electronics, new energy, semiconductor, industrial automation, and technical hardware.
- Global Trade Compliance Manager, Supply Chain Director, Strategic Sourcing Manager, Procurement Director, Logistics Manager, or trade advisor.
- Strongest pain: researching unfamiliar routes before a purchase order, quote, or shipment.

## LinkedIn Company Description

TraceWize helps US-based teams pre-screen compliance risks across global trade routes. Select a product, origin, and destination to review source-backed regulatory, tariff, product, and documentation signals before purchasing or shipping. TraceWize provides preliminary decision support and does not replace qualified customs, legal, or filing advice.

## First 10 LinkedIn Posts

1. **The positioning post:** Your office may be in the US while your goods move from Korea to Mexico, China to Europe, or Germany to Vietnam. Trade tools should follow the transaction—not the office address.
2. **Route lesson:** One product can face different duties, product rules, and evidence requirements when either its origin or destination changes.
3. **Third-country trade:** A shipment does not need to enter the US to become a decision for a US compliance or sourcing team.
4. **Before the PO:** Five questions to answer before approving a new overseas supplier: classification, origin, destination rules, special trade measures, and missing evidence.
5. **China → EU scenario:** What a US sourcing team should pre-screen before buying an energy storage system from China for delivery in Europe.
6. **Germany → US scenario:** What changes when an industrial robot enters the United States rather than another production market.
7. **Korea → Mexico scenario:** A practical example of third-country trade managed by a US headquarters team.
8. **Trust post:** Good compliance software should show what it knows, where the information came from, and where professional verification is still required.
9. **Supplier evidence:** The fastest compliance improvement may be asking the supplier for better product facts before classification begins.
10. **Invitation:** Share one technical product and one global route. We are inviting a small group of US-based teams to test a source-backed preliminary screen.

## Outreach Sequence

### Message 1 — connection or short email

Subject: A global route pre-screen for one of your products

Hi {{first_name}}, I’m building TraceWize for US-based teams that manage products moving across global routes—including third-country transactions where the goods never enter the US.

If you share one technical product, origin, and destination, I’ll prepare a free preliminary compliance screen showing potential tariff, product-rule, and documentation questions. I’d value your feedback on whether it fits your sourcing or trade workflow.

### Follow-up 1 — three business days later

Hi {{first_name}}, one example is a US sourcing team evaluating an energy storage system moving from China directly to the EU. TraceWize keeps the product, origin, destination, official sources, and missing evidence in one pre-screen. Would one current route be useful to test?

### Follow-up 2 — five business days later

Hi {{first_name}}, I’ll close the loop here. If global route research is part of your work, I can send a sample report or run a no-cost screen for one transaction. No product upload is required; a non-confidential description is enough for the first pass.

## 30-Day Execution Checklist

- Publish and QA `us-market.html`.
- Create three screenshots or short recordings from the linked scenarios.
- Add a demo-request or work-email capture endpoint before paid traffic.
- Recruit two licensed customs brokers or trade advisors as product reviewers.
- Build a list of 150 US-based prospects matching the ICP.
- Send 15–20 personalized messages per business day.
- Publish two route-specific LinkedIn posts per week.
- Run at least 10 real product/route screens.
- Record route, role, company type, result usefulness, repeat intent, and willingness to pay.

## Metrics

- Landing page → route screen started.
- Route screen → report generated.
- Report → demo requested.
- Qualified reply rate to outbound messages.
- Number of real routes screened.
- Repeat use by the same company.
- Professional-review corrections and missing-data patterns.
- Paid pilot or report willingness.

Do not buy traffic until the landing page has a functioning work-email/demo conversion and at least five qualified users have completed a real screen.
