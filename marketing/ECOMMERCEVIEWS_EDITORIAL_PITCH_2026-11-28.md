## Pitch email draft

**To:** editor@ecommerceviews.com  
**Subject:** Article pitch: The product-data handoff behind EU e-commerce customs identifiers

Hello Ecommerce Views editor,

I’m Carey Chen, founder of TraceWize, an early-stage product-and-market pre-screen for cross-border consumer-electronics sellers. I’d like to pitch an original, practical article for ecommerce operators about the EU product-identifier requirement that took effect on 1 November 2026 for imported distance sales with an intrinsic value not exceeding €150.

**Proposed headline:** “Three product identifiers, three owners: preparing product data for EU e-commerce customs declarations”

The article would explain, in plain operational terms:

1. Why a seller’s unique marketplace/store reference, the manufacturer’s model/part number, and a standardized identifier such as an EAN/UPC are distinct fields—not substitutes for one another.
2. How the data handoff can break between a seller’s product variant, supplier packaging/invoice, marketplace listing, and customs representative—and a small seller’s pre-shipment check to catch mismatches.
3. What the rule does not establish: it is not a product-safety certificate, product approval, or a complete customs-compliance check; and sellers should confirm the data format and exception handling with their declarant/platform.

I would cite the European Commission’s 3 November 2026 overview and 5 October 2026 member-state/trade guidance, keep the scope to qualifying imported distance sales, and avoid presenting TraceWize as a filer, identifier verifier or legal adviser. The piece would be written as an independently useful operational explainer, with my affiliation disclosed and no product CTA.

Would this topic and proposed scope suit your readers? If so, please let me know the target length and any rights, attribution, fee, or republishing terms I should review before preparing a full draft.

Best,
Carey Chen
