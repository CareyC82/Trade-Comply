## Today's original post — DRAFT, NOT PUBLISHED

> Selling a rechargeable product that will ship with a lithium battery? Ask for the UN 38.3 **test summary**, not only a one-line “battery is certified” statement.
>
> A useful first check is whether the summary identifies the actual cell/battery or product model, manufacturer, test report number and date, test facility, and battery details such as chemistry and watt-hour rating. Then compare those identifiers with the battery in the product you plan to ship.
>
> A summary that names a different pack—or omits the battery model or Wh rating—may not give the supply chain enough traceability. Ask the manufacturer or distributor to clarify before booking transport.
>
> Important boundary: UN 38.3 concerns lithium-battery transport testing. It is not, by itself, proof that the finished product meets every U.S. product-safety, FCC, or marketplace requirement. PHMSA guidance: https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/lithiumbatteries
>
