## Source check

- FTC guidance applies to U.S.-origin claims in product labels, advertising, and digital/social media. An unqualified “Made in USA” claim requires the product to be “all or virtually all” made in the U.S.; the FTC describes this as U.S. final assembly/processing, all significant processing in the U.S., and all or virtually all components made and sourced in the U.S., with a reasonable evidentiary basis.
- The FTC says a company’s U.S. address or a brand name alone ordinarily is not a U.S.-origin claim; nevertheless, the overall impression of marketing matters.
- FTC advertising/labeling analysis is distinct from CBP country-of-origin marking. The FTC notes that a CBP marking determination does not automatically establish that a Made-in-USA advertising claim is permissible.

**Imported electronics and “Made in USA” claims: a quick evidence check**

Before putting “Made in USA” on an electronics listing or package, map the claim to the product—not just the U.S. company address or warehouse.

For an unqualified “Made in USA” claim, the FTC’s standard is “all or virtually all” made in the United States. Its guidance looks at where final assembly or processing happens, whether significant processing occurs in the U.S., and where the product’s components are made and sourced. The claim also needs a reasonable evidentiary basis.

A practical supplier-record check:
• Which exact model/SKU does the claim cover?
• Where does final assembly or processing take place?
• Which components or processes are significant, and where do they occur?
• Would a supplier or production change make the claim stale?

This is not the same question as CBP’s country-of-origin marking requirement. The FTC notes that a CBP marking decision does not by itself authorize an unqualified “Made in USA” marketing claim.

Which fact is hardest to verify across suppliers: component origin, assembly location, or whether the production setup changed between batches?
